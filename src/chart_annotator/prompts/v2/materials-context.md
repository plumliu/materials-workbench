The source charts come from Ti-6Al-4V materials handbooks and may describe wrought or cast products. Product forms include sheet, strip, plate, bar, forging, wire, extrusion and casting. Use the current figure title, caption, axis titles, legend, specimen notes and visible geometry together. Do not infer the chart structure or the meaning of an abbreviation from one token alone.

MA means mill anneal or mill annealed, RA means recrystallization anneal, BA means beta anneal, STA means solution treatment and aging, STOA means solution treatment and overaging, ELI means extra low interstitial, and HIP means hot isostatic pressing. Master heat means metal from one furnace charge. Alpha case is the brittle contaminated surface layer formed during casting. These terms normally describe a material state, process or series condition. They are not numerical coordinates. Temperatures, times and pressures printed nearby are process parameters and do not create a universal numerical definition for an abbreviation.

Longitudinal and transverse describe specimen orientation relative to a product processing direction. For sheet or strip directionality charts, rolling direction is usually the reference direction. When an axis explicitly represents specimen orientation, test direction or direction relative to rolling, and its ordered labels contain L, intermediate numerical angles and T, L may represent 0 degrees and T may represent 90 degrees. For example, an ordered axis labelled L, 30, 60, T may be interpreted as the numerical sequence 0, 30, 60, 90 when the label positions, intermediate values, caption and geometry all support one linear angular mapping. Preserve the literal visible labels as evidence even when using their numerical values for calibration.

L and T may instead be legend conditions, table orientation fields or series names. When a figure only compares discrete longitudinal and transverse results and provides no continuous angle evidence, keep them as categorical or series conditions. LT, TL, L-T, T-L and S-L are compound specimen orientation codes; do not split them into separate 0-degree and 90-degree values. Long transverse and short transverse describe product or specimen geometry directions and must not automatically become numerical angles.

In cast-product charts, longitudinal, long transverse and short transverse often identify specimen extraction directions or curve conditions. Do not convert them to numerical angles unless the axis explicitly represents a continuous angle and the current figure's text and geometry support that conversion.

The same symbol can have different meanings in different figures. For example, R may mean a width-to-thickness strain ratio or a fatigue load ratio; it must not default to radial direction. Resolve a symbol from the current axis title, formula, legend and caption.

Distinguish coordinate axes from series conditions and specimen or process notes. Text inside the plotting area is not automatically an axis label, and a nearby number is not automatically a tick value. Preserve directly visible text and position before interpreting it. Convert a symbolic label into a numerical value only when the current caption, title, ordered label sequence and chart geometry support the mapping. When the available evidence does not resolve the meaning, report it as unresolved instead of guessing.
